1. Introduction

The Red Cross Society — Blood Donation Management System is an enterprise-oriented web application designed to manage, streamline, and centralize the operational workflow of regional blood banks, voluntary donor drives, and emergency transfusion requirements. Built on modern web architecture using PHP (Laravel Framework) and MySQL, the application draws its design principles directly from the national blood transfusion portal, e-RaktKosh.

In life-saving situations, locating verified, matching blood components (Whole Blood, PRBC, Platelet Concentrate, Fresh Frozen Plasma, and Cryoprecipitate) is time-critical. Traditional, localized blood registries rely on manual paper logging, unscheduled physical visits, and uncoordinated emergency calls. This lack of centralized visibility often leads to critical delays in patient care.

This system bridges that gap by offering a transparent, searchable, and secure public platform. The system supports three primary user roles:

  • Voluntary Donors: Can check their clinical donation intervals, locate upcoming blood donation camps, and register for digital donor certificates.
  • Hospital Attendants & Emergency Recipients: Can check real-time regional stock and submit urgent patient requisitions directly to the central blood bank.
  • Blood Bank Transfusion Officers (Admin): Can manage barcoded unit inventories, verify clinical physical screening vitals, and approve blood requests through a multi-stage clinical workflow.

By applying Model-View-Controller (MVC) architectural separation and Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), the project delivers high performance, secure session management, and relational data integrity.

1.1 Existing System

In traditional and paper-bound blood bank centers, operations rely heavily on manual recordkeeping and decentralized communication:

  • Manual Registry Logbooks: Blood units collected from donors are recorded by hand in physical paper registers. Staff must manually record donor contact information, collection dates, and component division logs.
  • Physical Counter Enquiries: Family members and hospital attendants must visit individual blood banks or call multiple facilities to verify whether specific blood groups are available in stock.
  • Paper-Based Pre-Donation Forms: Donors fill out physical medical intake forms to record preliminary physical checks (weight, hemoglobin, blood pressure, pulse). These paper records are prone to wear, physical misplacement, or accidental destruction over time.
  • Uncoordinated Emergency Requests: When an emergency occurs at a hospital, physical requisition slips signed by doctors are carried between hospital departments and blood centers, resulting in administrative bottlenecks during acute emergencies.

1.2 Limitations of the Existing System

The manual blood management process presents several operational challenges for both medical staff and patients:

  1. Time-Consuming & High Operational Latency: Manual ledger checks increase wait times for life-saving component transfusions.
  2. Absence of Centralized Inventory Visibility: Attendants cannot review regional multi-center inventory from a single interface.
  3. Risk of Data Loss & Wear: Paper records deteriorate, take up significant storage space, and cannot be backed up securely in the cloud.
  4. Vulnerability to Clinical Oversights: Manual methods can miss minimum donation cooldown periods (90 days for males and 120 days for females) or fail to flag expiring blood bags in time.
  5. Lack of Role-Based Security: Paper logs lack password encryption, access control checks, or tamper-proof audit trails for inventory adjustments.

2. Proposed System

The proposed web application replaces manual logging with a secure, centralized portal. Designed around the public health standards of e-RaktKosh, it automates live inventory updates, donation intervals, and medical requisitions.

  • Live Inventory Tracking: Displays cleared, unexpired blood bags across all major blood types (A+, A-, B+, B-, AB+, AB-, O+, O-) as well as rare groups like the Bombay Phenotype.
  • Barcoded Unit/Batch Identification: Tracks individual bags using unique serial barcodes (unit_barcode), recording bag capacity (350ml or 450ml), mandatory screening clearance (HIV, Hepatitis B/C, Malaria, Syphilis), and shelf-life expiration dates.
  • Algorithmic Cooldown Engine: Enforces gender-based clinical recovery intervals (90 days for male donors, 120 days for female donors) before allowing donation scheduling.
  • Digital Donor Identification: Generates a unique, trackable donor certificate reference (CERT-RCS-XXXXXX) for verified donors.
  • Multi-Stage Hospital Requisition Pipeline: Moves patient requests systematically across clinical stages: Pending → Cross-Match Verified → Approved / Issued → Fulfilled / Rejected.
  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Restricts administrative functions (such as stock issuance and workflow changes) to authorized staff via encrypted login credentials.

2.2 Objectives

  1. Digitize Blood Bank Records: Replace paper logs with structured MySQL relational tables for inventory, donor history, and requisitions.
  2. Provide Real-Time Public Stock Visibility: Offer an accessible search interface where hospital staff and attendants can filter blood units by group, component, and district.
  3. Enforce Clinical Safety Standards: Implement automated server-side checks for age (18–65), vital metrics, and mandatory post-donation cooldown intervals.
  4. Streamline Emergency Requests: Enable hospital attendants to submit digital blood requisitions, reducing the need for physical travel during emergency cross-matching.
  5. Secure Administrative Functions: Use dedicated middleware to restrict inventory approvals, state transitions, and drive scheduling to authorized medical officers.

5. Future Enhancements

  1. Automated GS1 Barcode Label Generation: Integrate barcode and QR code label printing for collected blood bags so phlebotomy staff can scan units into inventory using handheld scanners.
  2. Automated SMS & WhatsApp Alerts: Use cloud communication APIs (such as Twilio or Fast2SMS) to notify donors when their cooldown period expires and alert recipients when emergency requests are approved.
  3. Interactive Geo-Location Stock Locator: Integrate OpenStreetMap or Google Maps to help hospital attendants view the nearest blood bank with matching stock and get directions during urgent transfers.
  4. Dedicated Cross-Platform Mobile Application: Build a mobile companion app using Flutter or React Native to let voluntary donors view donation histories and download digital donor cards on their phones.
  5. Multi-Language Regional Localization: Add multilingual support (Gujarati, Hindi, and English) to improve access across regional communities.
